When you are giving interviews in companies like Amazon, Oracle, Wells Fargo, Goldman Sachs, BMW Group, Atlassian for a Data Engineer role — Use the below key points to clear the interview:

Here are 3 key takeaways from the experience:
- Big companies don't focus on memorized queries. They evaluate how you think and solve problems.
- Knowing tools is just the starting point — what matters is explaining why you choose a particular approach or technology.
- End-to-end ownership is expected — from data ingestion to transformation, governance, and optimization.

This experience taught me more than several shortlists ever did. Here's a quick breakdown of how the interviews went:

Round 1: Data Modeling & SQL
- Solving top-N SQL problems
- Designing an e-commerce data model
- Normalization vs denormalization concepts
- Composite keys and performance tuning
- Slowly Changing Dimensions (SCDs)

Round 2: Big Data & Distributed Systems
- Designing pipelines for high-volume data (1TB+/day)
- When to use Hadoop, Spark, or Flink
- S3 optimization strategies and Hive partitioning
- Data processing using Glue and EMR

Round 3: AWS & Cloud Architecture
- Redshift performance optimization
- ETL orchestration using Lambda and Step Functions
- Security, governance, and real-time ingestion patterns
- Design decisions around Kinesis and Kafka

Although I didn't clear all the rounds, I wasn't discouraged. Instead, I gained clarity on exactly what areas I need to strengthen.

What stood out most was that these interviews reward:
- Strong fundamentals
- Structured thinking
- Clear communication of design choices

That realization itself made me a better engineer.
