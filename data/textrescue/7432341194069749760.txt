Scenario-based SQL interviews don't fail on SQL.
They fail on unspoken assumptions.

If you've ever gotten the "right" query and the "wrong" answer, you already know why.

Most candidates start typing the moment they hear the prompt.

The problem isn't query-writing.

It's problem-definition.

Assumption: the interviewer wants syntax.
Reality: they're watching how you pin down meaning.

-You don't confirm grain before joining (user vs session vs order vs line item).
-You treat "missing" as a filter problem instead of a join problem (LEFT JOIN + null logic).
-You reach for NOT IN and forget that NULLs change the truth table.
-You use MAX(date) and think you retrieved the latest row, not just the latest value.
-You bucket time without stating boundaries (inclusive/exclusive, timezone, partial periods).
-You use window functions, but can't justify the partition/order as a business rule.

Average candidates produce outputs.
Great candidates produce definitions that survive edge cases.

Skip definition control and the costs stack up:

-inflated counts from duplicate joins
-disappearing entities from the wrong join type
-"correct" logic that breaks on NULLs
-time-based answers that drift month to month

Stop thinking in "write the query." Start thinking in "lock the contract."

Scenario SQL is data modeling under time pressure.
The interviewer is testing whether your query encodes the right assumptions (on purpose).
